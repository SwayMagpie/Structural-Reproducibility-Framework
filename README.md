### *From Physical Laws to Representational Algorithms*

**“Reproducibility as a Structural Basis for Cognition:  
From Physical Laws to Representational Algorithms.”**

**Zenodo DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23259601](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23259601)

The work reconstructs foundational concepts in cognition, computation, and language —  
**kichi/michi (known/unknown), algorithms, symbol manipulation, representational states** —  
through a single unifying principle: **reproducibility**.

---

## 🔧 **Core Idea**

Cognition is modeled as **ordered state‑transition sequences** grounded in two layers of reproducibility:

### **1. Physical Law Layer (E)**  
Reproducibility emerges from the triad:  
- **Structure** — finite interaction possibilities  
- **Interaction** — causal constraints  
- **Energy** — cost driving transitions  

This yields stable physical trajectories.

### **2. Representational Layer (I)**  
Biological sensors cannot map trajectories; they map **only states**.  
Thus reproducibility becomes **partial**:  
only *non‑breaking transitions* between representational states are guaranteed.

This partial reproducibility defines:

- **kichi (known)** — reconstructible entity  
- **michi (unknown)** — non‑reconstructible or unobserved entity  
- **algorithms** — reconstructible state sequences  
- **symbol / language operations** — socially constrained reproducible sequences
